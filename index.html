WHY DO WE PLAY?
A People have always played games. Archaeologists have found board games in ancient tombs that are more than five thousand years old, which shows that our ancestors enjoyed the same kind of pleasure we do today. Some of these games were played with dice, others with stones moved on a wooden board.

B Games are also a good way to learn. Children who play board games practise counting, planning and taking turns, while video games can improve quick thinking. Teachers sometimes ask students to play a game in class because it helps them remember new words, and nobody feels bored.

C Team sports teach different lessons. When players train together, they learn to support one another and to respect the referee's decisions, even if they lose. A good captain encourages the whole team, and a good coach knows that taking part is as important as winning.

D However, games can also cause problems. Some people spend too many hours on screens and give up other activities, such as sports or reading. Others cheat in order to win, which spoils the fun for everyone. Experts advise players to set a time limit and to remember that a game should be played for fun.

E In the future, games will probably become even more popular. New technology already allows players on different continents to compete against each other in real time, and more schools are starting clubs where students can try new games. Whatever the style, the pleasure of playing remains the same.
